# AB_DESIGN.md — the production experiment

`RESULTS.md` is a counterfactual simulation and proves nothing about users
(ARCHITECTURE §7, PROJECT_BRIEF §5b). Before the model's schedule goes live
for anyone, it runs as the experiment below. This file is the design; the
power table at the end is GENERATED by `make power` from the pinned
baseline rates (never edit inside the markers).

## Hypothesis

Sending the daily prompt at the model's schedule (`scores_send_time`,
cohort band + bounded per-user shift) raises the on-time rate per delivered
prompt versus the current fixed cohort hour, without raising opt-outs.
The simulation (`RESULTS.md`, medium) says the lift comes from the
per-user shift, not the band move alone (`cohort` arm 0.457732 vs baseline
0.460920; `recommended` 0.623291) — so the treatment is the full served
schedule, and a band-only arm is not worth a slot.

## Randomisation unit: user, within cohort

- **Unit.** The user (`user_id`), assigned once by a salted hash of the id
  into `treatment` / `control` / `holdout`, so the assignment is stable
  across days and devices and never re-drawn.
- **Within cohort.** The product's shared moment is per cohort, so the
  treatment is a cohort's schedule. Users are randomised *within* each
  cohort, and both arms keep a shared moment: control keeps the cohort's
  current fixed hour; treatment gets the model's band anchor plus its
  per-user shift (within `MAX_USER_SHIFT_MIN`). Cohort-level randomisation
  is rejected — three cohorts is not a sample.
- **Split.** 47.5 % treatment, 47.5 % control, 5 % holdout (below).

## Persistent holdout

5 % of users never receive the model's schedule, for the life of the
feature — not only the test window. Once the rollout is at 100 % the
contemporaneous control is gone; the holdout is the only remaining measure
of the long-run effect and of drift in the model. Its cost is its own
worse on-time rate, which is why it is small.

## Primary metric (pre-registered)

`ontime_rate` exactly as defined in [METRICS.md](METRICS.md#ontime_rate):
grain `(cohort_id, prompt_date)`, denominator `prompts_delivered`. The
experiment's estimand is the difference in `ontime_rate` between treatment
and control over the test window, pooled over cohort-days with
`prompts_delivered` as weights. Nothing here restates the metric; the
mart is the source.

## Guardrails (pre-registered, stop the rollout if breached)

- Notification opt-outs and unsubscribes per user-day — no increase beyond
  the noise band of the pre-period.
- Response rate per delivered prompt (`on_time + upload_fault`, the share
  that responded at all) — must not fall; the model may trade lateness
  for silence if the band is wrong.
- `delivery_fault` share — unchanged by design (the schedule does not touch
  delivery); a change signals an instrumentation problem, not an effect.
- `unattributed` share below `unattributed_max` in both arms.

## Send-time jitter

Once every user is sent at the model's hour, the data holds no other hour
and the model can never learn that another would be better (the exposure
bias ARCHITECTURE §2.8 names). In treatment, 10 % of sends, chosen per
user-day by the same salted hash, are shifted by a uniform ± 15 min. The
jitter stays inside the band, is logged on `prompt_sent`
(`event_properties.jitter_min`), and gives the model a continuous natural
experiment at a bounded cost.

## Duration and power

Run until the smaller arm has the delivered prompts the table gives for
the chosen MDE, and at least 14 days (two weekly cycles) whichever is
longer. One prompt per user-day; the delivered share is the profile's
`prompts_delivered / prompts_sent`. Two-sided α = 0.05, power = 0.8, the
two-proportion normal approximation (`eval/power.py`; users per arm =
`(z₁₋α/₂ + z₁₋β)² · (p₁(1−p₁) + p₂(1−p₂)) / (p₂ − p₁)²`). `days` assumes
half the profile's users in each arm. The `tiny` rows (20 users →
thousands of days) are the `POWER_TABLE` regression pin, not a plan; read
the `medium` rows for scale.

## Analysis plan

- Difference in `ontime_rate` (treatment − control) with a two-proportion
  z-interval over delivered prompts; a per-cohort breakdown as a secondary
  read, never a primary.
- Per-cause counts beside the rate, in the shape of `RESULTS.md`: the
  experiment should move `timing_gap` and leave `delivery_fault` alone.
- No peeking: one read at the pre-registered end; guardrails are monitored
  daily and can only stop, never extend.
